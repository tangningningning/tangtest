2. System model
We consider a highway scenario depicted in Fig. 1. We divide the 
system time into discrete time intervals of equal length and denote them 
by T ={1, 2,..., T}. Each time slot t∈ T has a fixed length, which is 
denoted as τ. The value of τ is determined by the coherence block of the 
wireless channel [6]. In a steady state, the maximum number of vehicles 
is N and the vehicle set on the road segment isN ={1, 2, ⋯, N}. The 
maximum communication range radius of a vehicle n in N for V2V is χ 
meters. A task generated by a vehicle n in time slot t can be represented 
as Dn(t) = (dn(t),cn(t),δn(t)), where dn(t) (bit) is the input data size of the 
task,cn(t) (cycles/bit) is the computation density, and δn(t) (s) is the 
maximum tolerable delay. Given the longer vehicle communication link 
duration between vehicles moving in the same direction, our focus is 
exclusively on vehicles travelling in the same direction. There are M 
RSUs uniformly deployed along the edge of the highway to enhance 
communication coverage range and signal quality. The communication 
range radius of an RSU m in RSU setM ={1, 2, ⋯, M} is assumed to be ε

1. Introduction
With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence and commu-
nication technology, transportation systems have entered the stage of 
intelligent vehicle-infrastructure cooperative system (i-VICS) [1]. In 
i-VICS, it is crucial to facilitate seamless information exchange among 
traffic participants and achieve a comprehensive understanding of the 
traffic environment. This necessitates collecting traffic information from 
various sensors installed on vehicles and roadways. The collected data, 
which is massive in scale, requires rapid processing and analysis. 
However, the computational limitations of individual vehicles pose a 
challenge in handling such vast amounts of data. Vehicular edge 
computing (VEC) emerges as a promising solution to this challenge [2]. 
By relocating computational capabilities to the edge of the network, VEC 
enables decentralized and localized execution of computationally 
intensive tasks from vehicles. Moreover, it alleviates the burden on the 
network backbone, enabling the provision of low-latency i-VICS
